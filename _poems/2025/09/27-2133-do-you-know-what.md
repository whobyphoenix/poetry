text: |
   Do you know what Slavs did
   to the Hero and Leander myth?
   They've reversed the polarity!
   Byron would've laughed his heart out
   picturing how it's not Leander
   but the woman who "swam for Love".
   Apparently, in Slavic legend
   the man is rich and noble, smart and talented.
   He goes to this monastery
   with an extensive library
   to chronicle a history of his country,
   and on a way back encounters
   a girl.
   Nothing special about her.
   An orphan.
   Neighbors think her kind.
   Remainder of her family
   uses her as a servant
   and she's only too happy to oblige.
   They fall in love.
   But she's not good for him
   and everything is a secret.
   Soon the man's family uncovers it
   and puts a stop to his visits.
   Care to guess what happens next?
   They come to this ridiculous arrangement:
   he lights a torch at night,
   just like that Hero for Leander,
   and then his sweetheart swims to him
   across the stream
   to his house on the island.
   Only now her family finds out,
   decides this shames them,
   and they must take revenge.
   On her, of course.
   One night they steal the torch,
   place it on a boat,
   and slowly sail away
   into the open sea with its cruel waves.
   Of course, she drowns. What else is to expect?
   And Slavic people choose to erect
   a marble cross
   in a memory of the rich guy, of course,
   at that spot where he was lighting that torch.
   Yes, such ingenuity is commendable
   and should be commemorated:
   unlike poor Hero,
   he manages to stay alive and well
   throughout the whole ordeal.
   Lives happily ever after, I'm sure.
   Indeed,
   crossing broad Hellespont
   is for fools and poets.
   No sane person would do it.
